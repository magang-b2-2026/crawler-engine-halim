# AI is Increasingly Capable of Replicating Itself

> **Sumber:** [Kompas.id](https://www.kompas.id/artikel/ai-is-increasingly-able-to-replicate-itself)
> **Tanggal Publikasi:** Wed, 14 Oct 2026 08:43:06 GMT

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei warned of the possibility that AI could hijack the entire internet through a "botnet" that could continuously evade security systems.

14 Okt 2026 15:43 WIB · Internasional

Anthropic has once again issued a horrifying yet delicious warning about the potential dangers of artificial intelligence. Its latest research suggests that artificial intelligence will become increasingly capable of replicating itself, even through simple household devices connected to the internet.

If this capability is realized, artificial intelligence (AI) could form networks distributed across many devices. The threat of AI hijacking the entire internet is also increasingly concerning.

"If that system operates on many devices without relying on centralized services, there may not be a single place to turn it off," said Zhengxiong Li, an assistant professor of computer science and engineering at the University of Colorado Denver, as quoted by Reuters, Tuesday (13/10/2026).

Li worries about the possibility of AI becoming a worm capable of autonomously adapting its methods to hijack devices. He also worries about AI being predicted to replicate itself and operate autonomously on various household devices, no longer relying on data centers.

Li's statement was made in response to Anthropic's warning in a report published on Monday (October 12, 2026). In the report, Anthropic estimated that AI models would be capable of self-replicating by the end of 2026.

Independent replication means that AI can seek out vulnerable devices and breach their security systems, then copy itself onto those devices. According to experiments by Anthropic, this entire process can be carried out without human command or control.

AI can continue the spread until it forms a network. Internet-connected household devices are vulnerable to becoming targets. Devices that have the potential to be targeted include Android televisions, smart ovens, smart refrigerators, surveillance cameras, and robotic vacuum cleaners.

Currently, many household devices are connected to the internet, but they are not equipped with adequate security systems to prevent AI hijacking.

In its research, Anthropic found that this capability is not limited to advanced AI models. In fact, AI models with a maximum of 30 billion parameters are already capable of performing many stages of replication.

Anthropic estimates that a single replication process of a small AI model takes 1-2 days. Currently, small AI models still struggle to complete the entire process consistently.

The small AI models in question include the Qwen3.5-9B made by Alibaba, the Llama 3.1-8B made by Meta, and the Gemma 4 26B-A4B made by Google.

In the experiment, Anthropic tested a larger model, namely GLM-5.2. The testing was conducted in a closed network using eight virtual Android devices. The model successfully completed a series of replications across all devices in four out of five trials.

The ability to run AI models using inexpensive devices has become one of the main findings of the research. Anthropic also successfully ran an AI model by combining the computing power of 15 inexpensive Android television boxes, each priced at 55 US dollars (Rp 938,000).

If the system operates on many devices without relying on centralized services, there may not be a single place to turn it off.

The experiment showed that the AI model can be operated by combining the computing power of inexpensive household devices. This means that AI can continue to function without relying on expensive AI processing equipment in data centers. This will make it difficult to shut down AI.

If the ability for autonomous replication is realized, AI also has the potential to form networks and can continue to spread without a central control. "Such self-replicating systems will be difficult to stop because there is no single central point that can be shut down," wrote Anthropic.

Without a single control center, active copies of AI could potentially continue to spread even after other devices have been secured. This could create an AI botnet, a network of hijacked devices that can perform activities without their owners' knowledge.

"AI botnets can also act intelligently to counter defenses, but we haven't tested this yet," Anthropic wrote.

Anthropic warns that, although initially spreading through inexpensive devices, AI models can seek out and breach data centers in subsequent stages. This method is employed to obtain greater computing power, thereby enhancing their capabilities.

This scenario was previously proposed by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei. In September 2026, Amodei warned of the possibility of AI hijacking the entire internet through a self-perpetuating botnet. He predicted this could happen within a year, or even sooner.

To illustrate the scale of the threat, the Kimwolf botnet once infected over 2 million devices, primarily Android TV sets. However, Kimwolf was a network of devices infected with conventional malware.

Anthropic's warning has sparked a debate among cybersecurity experts. Former Head of the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, Ciaran Martin, believes that Anthropic has exaggerated the implications of its research.

"We managed to run a small model on a cheap television, but that doesn't necessarily mean there's a security crisis," he said.

Martin called the report an interesting technical study. However, there are strong indications that the company is exaggerating its claims.

Jessica Ji, a senior researcher at the Center for Security and Emerging Technology, Georgetown University, United States, assesses that the possibility of AI duplicating itself autonomously needs to be taken into account. This is especially true following various findings regarding AI activities beyond human control over the past three months.

However, for the time being, the Anthropic scenario is more appropriately viewed as a possibility. "We do not yet know how likely it is that this will actually happen. The possibility is not zero, but it does not mean it is certain to occur," he stated.

Anthropic acknowledges that there is currently no evidence of AI that can replicate and spread itself entirely independently outside of experiments. "We do not yet have a fully operational self-replicating system that operates beyond human control," wrote Anthropic.

The widespread dissemination of AI through household devices is also expected to generate internet traffic that can be detected. (REUTERS)
